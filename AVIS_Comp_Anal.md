CHAPTER 4: Competitive Analysis
PURPOSE:
What is the segmentation and dynamics of the target market in terms of solution providers and supply?
This chapter evaluates the competitive environment in which the venture operates. It identifies direct, indirect, and substitute competitors, assesses how they solve the problem, and maps the venture’s relative positioning. The goal is to determine whether the startup has a sustainable differentiation, whether the market is contested or underserved, and whether the venture can secure a defensible position over time.
KEY SECTIONS:
Competitive Landscape Overview – Mapping of existing players and substitute solutions.
A list of market map of the top competitors or ecosystem players
Problem-defined framing: Who else solves the same core problem for the same customer?
Category-defined framing: Who plays in the same vertical or horizontal category?
Adjacency-defined framing: Who could pivot into this space given capabilities (e.g., platforms, capital, IP)?
Competitor Capabilities & Traction – Comparison of product features, customer adoption, pricing, and market share (SEE TABLE)
Positioning & Differentiation – The venture’s unique advantages or approach.
What whitespace is unclaimed or weakly defended?
Feature & Value Curve Matrix
Compare core features vs. level of differentiation
Highlight gaps (white space) and parity
Barriers to Entry & Moats – Structural, strategic, or product-level defensibility.
Moat Analysis Grid
Map each competitor's source(s) of defensibility: brand, data, switching cost, IP, network effects
Risk of Displacement or Obsolescence – Threats from incumbent innovation, new entrants, or changing conditions.
Threat Matrix
Show head-to-head risks: who beats your client on which dimensions?
Include fast-movers or stealth threats not yet at scale
Deal/Comps Tracker (for transaction purposes)
Past transactions, valuations, rationale, strategic buyers
Fit analysis: which competitors could be acquisition targets for whom, and why?
Who are realistic acquirers, and what gaps might they fill?

KEY QUESTIONS:
Who are the most relevant competitors?
What are key attributes used to analyze quality or purchase decisions in this segment?
What are the given relative strengths and weaknesses against these attributes against the competition?
How is the venture uniquely positioned to win - what is the alpha?
What barriers prevent others from copying the venture’s approach - what is the moat?
How do competitors price their offering and how does this offering compare?
Where is the competition most vulnerable?
DETAILED QUESTIONS:
Who are the top 3–5 direct competitors, and what is their market traction?
What product features or workflows do customers consistently praise or criticize in these competitors?
What is the venture’s functional or experiential advantage over alternatives?
Are there legacy incumbents with scale advantages, and how entrenched are they?
What are the key differentiators — pricing, ease of use, performance, speed of deployment, etc.?
How price-sensitive is the market, and where does the venture sit on the value/cost spectrum?
Are there substitute categories that solve the same problem in a different way (e.g., Excel, consultants, internal tools)?
What switching costs (technical, operational, or psychological) do competitors benefit from?
What proprietary elements (e.g., data, algorithms, channel access) are difficult to replicate?
How easily can a well-funded competitor replicate the venture’s current solution?
What indirect competitors could enter with superior distribution or adjacent offerings?
Are there emerging players or stealth startups gaining traction in the same space?
What partnerships, integrations, or ecosystems strengthen the venture’s competitive posture?
What external events (e.g., M&A, regulation, platform shifts) could dramatically reshape the landscape?
